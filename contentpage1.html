Early Developments

Pioneers such as Glenn Curtiss in 1917 and Constantinos Vlachos in 1935 attempted to fly us through the skies of the future but their attempts were futile due to the primitive technology available during the early 20th century (Popular Science, 1917, cited in Wikipedia, 2025).


Why aren't we commuting though the skies today? The world wasn't ready for such innovation, the publics trust was thin and the growing infrastructure was built for roads not runways.

Many people believe flying cars are a dream of the distant future - but the truth is that they’ve already taken flight in the 1940s. Post war, during the height of optimism, engineers believed the future soared across the skies.


In 1946, the design of the roadable aircraft began. This early Prototype utilized folding wings which allowed it to convert from a road vehicle to a plane in five minutes. (Glass, Andrew, 2015).

Year Manufacture Model Key Information
1917 Glenn Curtiss (USA) Curtis Autoplane Never achieved sustainable flight but was considered the first flying car. Proved the concept was feasible.(Popular Science, 1917, cited in Wikipedia, 2025).
1949 Molt Taylor Aerocar First functional flying car. could convert from car to plane in minutes. Recieved FAA certification. (Glass, Andrew, 2015, cited in Wikipedia, 2025).
1973 AVE (Advanced Vehicle Engineers, USA) AVE Mizar Ford Pinto cars body with the wings and tail of the Cessna Skymaster.(Popular Mechanics, 1973, cited in Wikipedia, 2025).
2013 AeroMobil (Slovakia) AeroMobil 3.0 Built with foldable wings, a true hybrid road air vehicle capable of practical flight. (CJ Hubbard, 2015)
2024 Kein Vision (Slovakia) AirCar Currently the most advanced street legal flying car. Certified for flight in Europe in 2022. (BBC News, 2024)
